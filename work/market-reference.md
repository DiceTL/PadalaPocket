# Competitors and alternatives

These are working research notes for Lili. We checked official product pages on 2026-11-07. This is a first pass, not a complete list or a test of the products. The short comparison lives in [seed/market.md](../seed/market.md); source records are in the [evidence ledger](evidence-ledger.md).

PadalaPocket’s team-selected audience is students aged 13 and older who receive allowance from an OFW parent. The accepted prototype covers one parent, one student and one demo seller on Testnet. Real peso/GCash settlement and multiple children are deferred. Those limits matter when comparing it with products people can use today.

## Local financial products

### GCash Jr.

GCash’s terms describe a verified minor’s account linked to a parent or guardian. The account can access supported GCash services, and the guardian is responsible for monitoring it. The setup guide lists identity and relationship documents and describes an application process.

This is a relevant option for a family already using GCash. The pages we reviewed don’t tell us how families use it for allowance or whether its controls meet their needs. They also don’t support saying that GCash Jr. lacks a feature PadalaPocket proposes.

**When a family might choose it:** if the parent and child already use GCash and mainly need a linked minor wallet with familiar payment services.

Sources: [terms](https://gcash.com/terms-and-conditions/gcash-jr), [setup guide](https://help.gcash.com/hc/en-us/articles/39417503859737-How-to-create-a-gcash-jr-account-for-your-child). Evidence: EV-026.

### BPI Jumpstart

BPI describes Jumpstart as a savings account for children and teens, with an adult accompanying the child to open it at a branch. Its pages mention an allowance-transfer facility and tools for savings goals and chores or rewards.

It may suit families looking for a child savings account and ways to encourage saving. We haven’t established whether its setup and tools work well for a parent managing allowance from abroad. Check current account requirements and fees before quoting them.

**When a family might choose it:** if a bank savings relationship, savings tools and branch-based account setup fit the family better than a separate allowance-management app.

Sources: [Jumpstart](https://www.bpi.com.ph/personal/bank/savings-accounts/jumpstart), [Jumpstart Savings](https://www.bpi.com.ph/personal/bank/savings-accounts/jumpstart-savings). Evidence: EV-027.

### Maya accounts for minors or students

Maya’s support page says minors or students aged 7–17 may qualify for an account upgrade. It lists child identification and parent or guardian consent documents.

This is another digital-account option. The page we checked is about eligibility and verification; it isn’t enough to compare Maya’s full allowance or spending-control features with PadalaPocket.

**When a family might choose it:** if they already use Maya and the student can meet its eligibility and verification requirements. This is a reasonable scenario, not user research.

Source: [Maya support](https://support.maya.ph/s/article/Can-I-upgrade-my-account-even-though-I-am-a-minor-or-a-student). Evidence: EV-028.

## Other ways families can manage allowance

### Remittance or bank transfer, then family-managed allowance

A family may send money through a bank, money-transfer service or wallet, then decide separately how much becomes student allowance and how to keep track of it. In the PSA's 2025 Survey on Overseas Filipinos, banks accounted for 63.1% of reported cash remittances and money-transfer services for 34.1% during April–September 2025 (see EV-030 and the [facts reference](facts-and-statistics-reference.md)). Those figures describe transfer channels; they do not show how families distribute allowance after receiving money.

This route may work well when the family already trusts its transfer service and is comfortable setting allowance rules separately. Tracking may happen in conversation, notes or a spreadsheet, but we have not established how often these methods are used or whether families find them difficult. Questions to investigate: which route a family already uses, who decides the allowance amount, and whether any part of the current routine is worth changing.

### Virtual allowance trackers

AllowanceKit describes scheduled virtual allowance and custom buckets, and says it does not connect to a bank or move real money. KidTab describes allowance and savings tracking without a bank connection or real-money transfers. Its site says children currently don’t have their own sign-in or in-app purchase-request flow.

These tools can help a family keep a shared record without setting up a financial account. They don’t execute payments. The examples we checked are international; we haven’t confirmed their availability or use in the Philippines.

**When a family might choose one:** if they mainly want a simple shared record or savings buckets and prefer to handle real cash or transfers outside the app.

Sources: [AllowanceKit](https://allowancekit.app/), [KidTab](https://kidtab.app/). Evidence: EV-029.

## Possible differences to investigate

The product concept combines remote allowance management, Needs/Wants rules, weekly handling of leftover funds, and a distinction between controlled allowance and the student’s My Money. These are features in the proposed product scope; the comparison below does not establish that families want them.

- **Managing allowance across distance:** Could be useful to an OFW parent, but a linked account, regular transfer, or family routine may already be sufficient.
- **Needs/Wants and savings structure:** May help a family organize funds, but separate accounts or a manual budget could do the same job for them.
- **Parent rules with student autonomy:** This is a design choice in PadalaPocket’s concept. We have no evidence yet that it improves budgeting or family relationships.
- **Payment versus record keeping:** A Testnet payment demo is different from a virtual tracker, but it does not establish real peso settlement, merchant coverage, or commercial readiness.

Treat these as hypotheses for discussion, not product decisions or pitch claims. A feature appearing in PadalaPocket and not on one competitor page does not prove unmet need.

## What we know and what we don’t

We have reviewed public product information; we have not opened accounts, tested these products, compared every fee, or measured use among OFW families. We also don’t know which products a particular family can access or prefers.

If the team wants to deepen the research, first check current eligibility, fees, parent controls and transfer steps for the options that matter to the intended audience. Interviews are not a required project gate. If the team chooses to gather user input, ask neutral questions about families’ current routines before presenting PadalaPocket’s proposed features.
