## Best current pitch context: 2025 Overseas Filipinos Survey

The PSA's nationwide 2025 Survey on Overseas Filipinos estimates **2.05 million OFWs** worked abroad during April–September 2025. In that same six-month reference period, total reported remittances were **₱240.73 billion**: ₱206.90 billion in cash sent, ₱27.64 billion in cash brought home, and ₱6.19 billion in kind. The PSA's 29 October 2026 advisory says the final results are the same as the preliminary results released on 9 October 2026.

**What this can support:** the scale of overseas work and family remittances in the Philippines. It gives the audience broader context for Jenelle and Aling Jam's illustrative story.

**What this cannot support:** the number of OFWs who have children in the target age group; how much of remittances becomes student allowance; whether OFW families have difficulty managing allowance; whether families want PadalaPocket; or the product's market size.

**Important definitions and limits:** The survey covers overseas workers who left during October 2020–September 2025 and worked abroad during April–September 2025. Household members in the Philippines provide information about the OFW. The survey does not ask for total OFW salary, so the reported remittances may represent only part of it. The ₱240.73 billion figure covers six months and includes cash sent, cash brought home, and goods; do not call it a yearly cash-transfer total.

Sources: [PSA 2025 results](https://psa.gov.ph/statistics/survey/labor-and-employment/survey-overseas-filipinos/node/1684084311), [technical notes](https://psa.gov.ph/system/files/iesd/SOF%202025%20Technical%20Notes.pdf), [PSA final-results advisory](https://psa.gov.ph/statistics/survey/labor-and-employment/survey-overseas-filipinos/index). Evidence: EV-030.

## Background only: adult budgeting and financial knowledge

The BSP's Philippines-wide 2025 Consumer Finance and Inclusion Survey says **86% of Filipino adults reported having a personal budget**. It also reports that **74% answered at least half of six basic financial-literacy questions correctly**.

The survey's qualified respondents were aged 15 and older. It had 8,784 completed interviews from a sample of 10,836 and weighted results to represent the Philippine adult population based on the 2020 Census. The budget figure is self-reported; the six-question result measures a small set of basic concepts.

This is not evidence about 13- and 14-year-olds, OFW-parent households specifically, or whether PadalaPocket would improve budgeting. It may be useful as context that many adults already report budgeting, which cautions against claiming that families lack budgeting practices. Unless the pitch needs that point, omit this statistic from the opening.

Source: [BSP 2025 Consumer Finance and Inclusion Survey](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Inclusive%20Finance/Financial%20Inclusion%20Reports%20and%20Publications/2025/2025CFISreport.pdf). Evidence: EV-031.

## Older PSA figures in the earlier script draft

The earlier spoken draft used 2.19 million OFWs and ₱262.20 billion in remittances for 2024. Those are valid 2024 figures, but PSA has since published the 2025 Survey results. Use the newer 2025 numbers if the pitch is meant to present the latest available survey result. Keep the 2024 numbers only when making a clearly labeled year-to-year comparison.

## Pitch wording to consider

Taglish draft:

> “Para sa mas malawak na context, ayon sa PSA, tinatayang 2.05 million ang OFWs na nagtrabaho abroad mula April hanggang September 2025. Sa parehong six-month period, umabot sa ₱240.73 billion ang total remittances—kasama rito ang cash na ipinadala, cash na iniuwi, at padalang goods. Ipinapakita nito ang laki ng overseas work at remittances sa Pilipinas, pero hindi nito sinasabi kung ilang pamilya ang may ganitong situation o kung kailangan nila ng app tulad ng PadalaPocket.”

Keep the small source note on the slide: **Source: Philippine Statistics Authority, 2025 Survey on Overseas Filipinos; reference period April–September 2025.**

If that feels too number-heavy for the story, use only the 2.05 million OFW estimate and put the remittance figure in speaker notes. Avoid combining the count and amount in a way that suggests every OFW sent the same amount or that the total was all cash sent to children.
