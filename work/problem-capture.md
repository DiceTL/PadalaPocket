# Idea development questions

Use these neutral prompts one topic at a time. They are prompts, not a compulsory questionnaire.

- What situation should this idea improve, and what is still unclear about it?
- Who is involved, and what outcome matters to each person?
- What happens today, and what evidence supports that description?
- Which parts of the draft should be retained, reconsidered, or removed, and why?
- What alternatives could produce the desired outcome? What are their tradeoffs?
- What must be decided to explain the concept consistently?

## Refinement notes — 2026-11-06

- **Firsthand account:** A parent worked in Dubai for about 11 months. The father at home, who also worked, gave the founder an allowance on school days; leftovers went into savings. Allowance was mostly fine. The founder does not know how the parents handled money between them. This account does not establish a failure in allowance delivery or a general market need.
- **Founder hypothesis:** An OFW parent may want to send allowance directly to children and see or limit its use. In the imagined family, money otherwise passes through a spouse, relative, or caregiver. Diversion, inappropriate purchases, and excessive discretionary spending are hypothetical concerns, not observed findings.
- **Segment concern:** The founder does not favor pursuing a single-parent OFW angle at present. The suggested rarity of that situation is an unverified intuition; caregiver involvement alone does not establish whether direct allowance is useful. No segment decision recorded yet.
- **Desired outcomes proposed:** Confidence that children receive allowance, visibility/control over spending, personal savings, and practice managing money. These remain candidates rather than accepted requirements or demonstrated product effects.
- **Open tension:** Parental monitoring and restrictions may limit independence. Clarify what choices the child would actually own and which outcome takes priority.
- **Question to resolve:** What should direct allowance improve for the family compared with the existing caregiver-mediated arrangement?
- **Founder clarification:** In the hypothetical family, the parent abroad primarily wants confidence that children receive their intended allowance and spend it appropriately. The proposed motivation is peace of mind; reduced stress or improved mental health is a hypothesized benefit, not an established effect. This refines the problem being explored without accepting a feature set.
- **Additional motivations to explore (agent proposals, recorded at founder request):** Less allowance administration through fewer repeated transfers/messages; fewer approval delays across time zones; clearer parent/caregiver/child agreement on what allowance covers; a consistent saving routine; and practice making choices within a budget. These are unvalidated hypotheses, not accepted requirements or demonstrated benefits.
- **Founder definition of appropriate spending:** Exclude alcohol, cigarettes, and similar harmful purchases; seek agreement for purchases not previously discussed, such as video games, instruments, or other expensive products. The examples do not establish a blanket ban on games or instruments. Exact restrictions, thresholds, and approval rules remain undecided.
- **Monitoring question:** What trusted information would identify the actual items before payment? The original draft proposes backend category/cap checks and simulated merchants, but does not establish a reliable source of item-level purchase data. Its over-limit approval timeout is a proposal, not a decided rule.

AI identifies the pictured product and checks category consistency. The parent sees the photo, identified product, transaction amount, and category to review plausibility. This is a candidate approach, not an accepted requirement or verified capability. Open issues include uncertain identification, mixed baskets, prohibited items within otherwise valid categories, and linking the photograph to what is actually purchased. Matching an image to a category does not verify the price, purchased item, or delivery.
