# Competitors and alternatives

Families already have options for giving children money: a linked wallet such as GCash Jr., a child savings account such as BPI Jumpstart, an eligible Maya account, or a bank/remittance transfer followed by a family-managed allowance routine. Some allowance apps only keep a virtual record and do not move money. A family might choose an existing wallet or bank if it already fits how they manage money; a tracker might suit a family that wants records but no app-based payment.

For context, the PSA’s 2025 survey reports that banks and money-transfer services are the main channels for cash remittances. That describes how money is sent, not how families decide or track a student’s allowance. We don’t yet know whether families want PadalaPocket’s combination of remote allowance management, agreed spending rules and separate personal funds. The comparison and sources are in [seed/market.md](../seed/market.md), with fuller notes in [work/market-reference.md](../work/market-reference.md).

For the pitch, describe this as a problem we’re exploring, not a gap we’ve proven. Our demo uses Testnet; real peso/GCash settlement is deferred and the fiat payout is simulated.
